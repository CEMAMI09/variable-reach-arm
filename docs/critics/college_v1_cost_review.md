# Independent compact V1 cost review — 8 September 2026

The independent critic agreed that a compact 350–550 mm reach V1 is plausible,
but rejected converting the current full-size arm to plastic as a major cost saving.
Their initial unitemized $280–420 parts envelope conflicted with the root's $547.86
line-item basket. On direct review of that basket, the critic withdrew the lower
estimate: no definite duplicate cost was found, and <=$420 was not defensible while
preserving powered yaw/pitch/extension/claw, feedback, pitch restraint and electrical
protection. Adopt $547.86 parts / $705.64 planning cash until specific reductions
are demonstrated. Do not average conflicting estimates or quote the optimistic one.

Major issues: unclear demonstration scope (high severity/high likelihood); long
printed boom joints and guide straightness (high/high); pitch collapse on power
loss (high/medium); unqualified actuator/ESP32 compatibility (high/high). Minor
issues include printer access and repair consumables (medium/medium), and
unproven upgrade reuse (medium/high). Retain shafts/bearings and wear components,
use shorter printed structures, and characterize fit before committing to a set.

The critic's independent favorable-to-ideal beam model at the current full length
estimated 0.339 mm aluminum inner-boom sag versus 9.39 mm for 3 mm-wall PETG using
1.5 GPa printed-coupon modulus, including inner-tube self-weight and catcher offset.
The root's uniform tip-load ratio uses a different supplier's higher 2.31 GPa XY
modulus and finds 18–19 times greater deflection at equal span. These are different
assumptions, not contradictory results; neither includes joints or creep and both
reject an equivalent-performance plastic substitution at the old span.

Compact V1 concept score: 5/10; not ready to order a full kit or manufacture.
Needs dedicated CAD, dimensional/actuator sizing and actual hardware interfaces.
Cheap fit coupons and isolated claw tests are the useful next prototype work.
